Colours show who ruled at that time, and each country has its own fixed colour. The stronger the colour, the firmer the rule; hatching means rule that is weakening or contested; a very pale colour means the country has left the contest for hegemony but still exists. Tap an area for its name and source; tap a country in the list below to keep only that country and follow its course and territory over time. ◆ marks strategic points such as straits, canals and bases; where the source map was wrong, the map has been recoloured with checked values.
Borders: up to 1880, historical-basemaps (André Ourednik et al., GPL-3.0) with year-by-year corrections; from 1914, redrawn from CShapes 2.0 (Schvitz, Girardin, Weidmann et al., dated borders and colonies). For 1914 onwards, where both sources exist, the ‘Cross-check’ section of each year shows the agreement rate and where they differ. Strategic points and some regions were recoloured with checked values where the source was wrong. Territory areas are approximations computed by sampling the final map on a 0.25° grid. Because the gaps between years are uneven, ‘Gains and losses’ covers the main events since the previous year shown. For 1492–1880, places where the boundary itself was wrong (e.g. Iraq, Tunisia and Hungary in 1530; Qing Mongolia and the Amur basin in 1700) were found by comparing with the independent Cliopatria dataset (historical borders from the Seshat team); only those confirmed against the historical record were recoloured, borrowing Cliopatria’s boundary. The same corrections are applied to the companion world territory map (Korean only). The two maps use different criteria, however: this atlas follows effective influence, colouring the British dominions (Canada, Australia, etc.) as British in 1914 and 1920, and Manchukuo and Ethiopia (1938) and Indonesia (1945) by the power actually in control, whereas the territory map follows legal sovereignty. Aragon in 1492 is shown as Spain here but as a separate state there, and the second-to-last year is 1994 here versus 1991 there.
