Research notes on the history of ideas

Turning Points of Thought

Seven moments, from the Scientific Revolution to the AI Revolution, when the standards humanity uses to judge truth, power, the human and the good life changed. Follow how standards shifted before and after each turning point, which isms were born, and how they inherited from or reacted against one another.

Turning points: what changed

Every turning point is compared on the same four axes: the basis of truth (what we take to be true), the legitimacy of power (who rules and why), the view of the human (what a person is) and the way of life (how we work and live). Tap an ism to open its details.

Ism flow chart

Columns follow the turning points; rows are the four domains. A bar shows when an ism was born and held influence; a fading end means decline, an arrow means it is still alive. Tap a bar to highlight its roots and descendants.

Swipe sideways to move through the eras. Columns are turning points, not equal spans of years, so recent columns cover shorter periods.

Era-by-era graphs

Each era argued about different things, so each graph has its own two axes: the two questions on which that turning point split most sharply. Filled dots are isms born in that era; hollow dots are older isms that took part in its debates.

Ism dictionary

Each ism with its core claims, key thinkers and works, and a one-line thesis. “Paraphrase” marks a sentence that restates the core idea rather than quoting the original.

How to read this, and its limits. Turning points are not clean cuts. Most ideas sprout just before a turning point and spread over the following decades, and some events placed later in the sequence, such as the Industrial Revolution (from the 1760s), actually began earlier. This framework is built around modern Western history and does not cover the independent turning points of Islamic, Indian or Chinese thought. Korea and East Asia absorbed these ideas in a compressed span from the late 19th century. The AI Revolution entries summarise debates still under way as of November 2026.
